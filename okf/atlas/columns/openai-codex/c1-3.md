## Answer

Covered. Approval policy must stay on-request or granular; auto-review can replace the human approver if enabled; hooks are a guardrail, not a complete enforcement boundary.

Source statement: "runs when Codex is about to ask for approval, such as a shell escalation or managed-network approval." (https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/hooks)

Research detail: Approval policies (on-request, never, granular) decide when Codex pauses for a person. The approval request carries the exact command or tool input, a PermissionRequest hook can allow or deny it, and after a denial the agent must find a safer path or stop and ask. Auto-review can replace the human with a reviewer agent.

## Products

Approval policies, PermissionRequest hooks, Auto-review

<!-- chunk: gaps -->
## Gaps

Replay handling and binding of an approval to the exact action are not documented. Auto-review substitutes an agent for a human.

<!-- chunk: options -->
## Options

Enforce on-request or granular approvals in requirements.toml and restrict allowed_approvals_reviewers where a human decision is required.
