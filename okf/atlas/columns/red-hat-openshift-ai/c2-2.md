## Answer

Covered. Models-as-a-Service on OpenShift AI 3.5 self-managed. Models are called by name through the gateway endpoint, with per-model token limits per subscription. External models are Technology Preview, and data handling for those depends on the external provider.

Source statement: "MaaS assigns users to subscriptions based on their OpenShift group membership." (https://docs.redhat.com/en/documentation/red_hat_openshift_ai_self-managed/3.5/html-single/govern_llm_access_with_models-as-a-service/index)

Research detail: Models are served on KServe with vLLM and other runtimes, or with llm-d distributed inference, inside the customer cluster. Deployments are versioned through the model registry and the dashboard shows the running vLLM version. Models-as-a-Service sets per-model token limits and routes to local or external models.

<!-- chunk: products -->
## Products

KServe single model serving, Red Hat AI Inference (vLLM), llm-d, Models-as-a-Service

<!-- chunk: gaps -->
## Gaps

External model egress is Technology Preview. Data handling for external providers depends on those providers.

<!-- chunk: options -->
## Options

Pin runtime images and record model version per deployment. Test that requests to an unlisted model are refused by MaaS.
